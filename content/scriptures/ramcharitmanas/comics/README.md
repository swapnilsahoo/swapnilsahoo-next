# Ramcharitmanas visual retellings

These assets are optional interpretive reading companions keyed to stable reader-unit IDs. They do
not alter the source corpus, supply textual evidence, or claim to reconstruct historical appearance.
The manifest deliberately calls the granularity `reader-unit`: the 1,074 numbered upstream records
contain unsplit combinations of caupāīs, dohās, sorathās, chhands, and ślokas and are not a universal
verse count.

Every entry must preserve its source-text and image SHA-256 hashes, dimensions, accessible alt text,
caption, generation provenance, and editorial review status. AI-assisted artwork remains visibly
labelled on the public reader and must never be described as human-generated or as part of Tulsidas's
text.

## Art direction · version 1

Refined Indian miniature-painting sensibility combined with restrained contemporary graphic-novel
storytelling; horizontal four-panel composition; consistent figures and palette; no embedded text,
logos, pseudo-Devanagari, or watermark. Exact scripture text and editorial meaning remain selectable
HTML outside the image.

The first asset was generated with OpenAI's built-in image-generation tool from a source-specific
prompt describing Bālakāṇḍa numbered unit 1: the guru's lotus-foot dust as fragrant pollen and healing
nectar, its affinity with Shiva's pure ash, and its jewel-like light revealing the episodes of Rama's
story. It was visually checked before integration. Its embedded provenance marker is retained and its
exact checksum is approved by `scripts/check-media-provenance.mjs`.

The second asset applies the same system to Bālakāṇḍa's first Sanskrit invocation, portraying Vāṇī
and Vināyaka through four symbolic beats: creative presence, sound gathering into meaning, poetic
sentiment and metre, and an auspicious blessing over a blank manuscript. It was generated with the
same built-in tool, visually checked, source-bound, and checksum-approved without removing its
embedded provenance marker.

The third asset interprets Bālakāṇḍa's second Sanskrit invocation: Bhavānī and Śaṅkara as faith and
trust, with disciplined contemplatives moving from a veiled heart-lotus to a quiet inward flame. The
same generation, visual-review, source-binding, disclosure, and checksum controls apply.

The fourth asset interprets Bālakāṇḍa's third Sanskrit invocation: the guru revered across time,
Shiva's compassionate presence in awakened understanding, and the crooked crescent receiving honour
because it rests in Shiva's matted locks. It follows the same four-panel art direction and provenance,
visual-review, source-binding, disclosure, and checksum controls.

The fifth asset interprets Bālakāṇḍa's fourth Sanskrit invocation: Sītā and Rāma's virtues as a sacred
forest, Vālmīki's purified poetic insight, Hanumān's devotional leadership, and their shared reverence.
The same four-panel art direction and provenance, visual-review, source-binding, disclosure, and
checksum controls apply.
