# Learning Lab — lean 90-day pilot

Private planning proposal, 8 October 2026. Day numbers begin only after the founder chooses a start date. Nothing below is an achieved result or authorisation to contact people. The first paid offer is proposed as **AI for Managers**, with 12 adults, four weeks, six 90-minute live sessions and roughly two independent learner hours each week. These are planning assumptions, not public availability.

## Days 1–15: learn before selling

Founder: arrange 12–15 consented discovery conversations through approved personal/business channels, including adult management learners, early-career professionals and 3–5 college or corporate learning decision-makers. Do not use an employer's student database or institutional email. Record needs in short de-identified notes, not recordings by default. Ask for the last concrete managerial task, what failed, current alternatives, time available, purchasing authority and acceptable evidence of learning. Do not pitch until the problem is understood.

Builder/instructor: test all three demonstration lessons with 5–6 volunteers. Observe completion, comprehension of instructions and ability to explain the output. Separate usability comments from evidence of improved judgement. Finalise one flagship problem; revise or stop if interest is only polite.

Gate: documented recurring problem, the founder's willingness to teach/support the pilot, a feasible access model and an approved discovery-data notice. Keep prices private until approved.

## Days 16–30: approve the offer and operating conditions

Approve the AI programme version, rubric, realistic capacity, delivery dates, fee/tax display, support hours, attendance rule and certificate wording. Proposed completion criteria: all six lessons complete, at least 80% live attendance and an original capstone approved by an instructor at 60/100 or more. Establish a fair accessible alternative for justified attendance barriers before accepting payment.

Complete the launch gates in [incorporation-and-launch.md](incorporation-and-launch.md). Assign a named founder/admin support owner; prepare an independently controlled business mailbox. Back up the database and practise a restore. Establish manual incident and learner-rights response procedures. Review the unit economics in [business-budget.md](business-budget.md); no payment collection while merchant, tax or policy review is incomplete.

Gate: founder approval recorded; no missing payment prerequisites; tested enquiry and invited learner flows; approved contact/privacy/refund information. A delay moves delivery dates rather than bypassing a gate.

## Days 31–45: recruit one cohort

After explicit outreach authorisation, send the reusable proposal only to appropriate institutional contacts and record responses truthfully. Use approved business channels. Do not promise employment, institutional credit, accreditation or AI automation. Offer a short sample decision exercise and explain the measurable capstone.

After payment has separately been implemented, configured and approved, invite qualified adults to the single paid pilot. Reconfirm the exact dates, fees, cancellation terms and workload before taking funds. Use hosted checkout, signed verified webhooks and idempotency if checkout is enabled. Until then, registration of interest is the only action and must not be counted as enrolment or revenue. Provision learner accounts individually; verify eligibility and consent without collecting dates of birth or identity documents unnecessarily.

Gate: enough confirmed learners for an economically and educationally viable cohort, or an explicit founder decision to fund a small research pilot. Do not expand capacity to repair weak margins.

## Days 46–75: deliver, support and assess

Run the six facilitated sessions over four weeks. Keep an attendance record, mark lesson completion and collect an initial decision memo before teaching. Give feedback on an original capstone using the same published rubric. Learners disclose permitted AI assistance; instructor feedback and certification remain human responsibilities.

Reserve founder support time twice weekly and aim to acknowledge requests within two working days once the support channel is staffed. This is a proposed internal service target, not an unattended website promise. Record material access problems and offer the approved accommodation. Do not record learner contributions by default. Obtain separate, specific consent if a recording is necessary, with an alternative for learners who decline.

Gate: each certificate has evidence of eligibility and instructor approval; progress alone does not prove competency. Record withdrawals, reasons, refunds and support incidents without turning personal comments into testimonials.

## Days 76–90: decide whether to repeat

Compare real outcomes against the targets below, reconcile payment/refund records where applicable and calculate margin including founder time. Interview completers and withdrawals. Request a separate opt-in before any testimonial or public identity use; silence is not consent. Revise the syllabus and offer terms, then decide to repeat, redesign or stop. Discuss a second programme only after the first has useful learning evidence and a workable support load.

## Measurement definitions

These are proposed targets for a first pilot, not forecasts or live dashboard values. Actuals remain **not yet measured** until real records exist. Synthetic demo data is excluded.

| Measure | Definition and denominator | Proposed pilot target | Actual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified enquiries | Adults matching audience, specific problem and feasible participation; deduplicated people, not form submissions. | 30–40 | Not measured |
| Conversion | Confirmed paid learners / qualified enquiries offered the approved cohort; report both counts. | 12 learners; around 30% if 40 offers | Not measured |
| Attendance | Live-session attendance minutes / available minutes, by learner; report approved alternatives separately. | Median at least 80% | Not measured |
| Completion | Learners meeting all published requirements / enrolled adults, including withdrawals in denominator. | At least 9 of 12 | Not measured |
| Learning improvement | Paired pre/post decision task scores on the same 100-point rubric; report sample size and missing pairs. | Median increase 15 points | Not measured |
| Capstone quality | Instructor-approved original capstones at 60/100 or above / submitted capstones. | At least 80% | Not measured |
| Satisfaction | Post-cohort overall usefulness, 1–5 scale; report response count and distribution. | Median 4/5; at least 8 responses | Not measured |
| Refunds | Refunded amount / gross collected fees; also learners refunded / paying learners and reasons. | Below 10%, with every valid request honoured | Not measured |
| Repeat purchase | Actual purchase of a later approved offer / eligible pilot learners; intent is reported separately. | Establish baseline; no invented target sales | Not measured |
| Institutional renewal | Signed and paid subsequent cohort / institutions that completed a first pilot. | Establish baseline; denominator may be zero | Not measured |

Small, self-selected cohorts, instructor scoring and missing post-tasks limit causal claims. Use anonymised task comparisons and, where feasible, a second assessor for a subset. Do not claim the pilot caused employment or business success. The MVP can report enquiries, progress, submissions and certificate records; attendance, paired assessment, satisfaction and payment outcomes need explicit records and a manual pilot scorecard until implemented.

## Founder operating checklist

Weekly: review enquiries and consent status; respond through the Lab's business channel; verify cohort capacity; check access/support issues; review and approve feedback; reconcile costs and any authorised receipts/refunds; check backups and privileged accounts; record real outcomes and decisions. Monthly: review retention/deletion, curriculum licences, conflict-of-interest boundaries, processor access and cash runway. At cohort close: complete fair reassessment, certificate audit, feedback review and a written repeat/stop decision.
