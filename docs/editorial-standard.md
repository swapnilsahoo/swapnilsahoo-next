# Editorial and source-integrity standard

This standard applies to every public page and teaching room on
`swapnilsahoo.com`.

## 1. Keep source material intact

Do not “humanize” sacred text, quotations, publication titles, credentials,
dates, statistics, archival records, or cited source passages. Preserve their
wording and identify the edition, recension, license, or evidence trail where
that context matters.

For Sanskrit works, keep these layers visibly separate:

1. received text in its original script;
2. scholarly transliteration;
3. editorial word division or compound analysis;
4. close literal meaning;
5. modern explanation or reflection.

Never present editorial interpretation as though it were part of the ancient
text. Record material variants instead of silently combining them.

## 2. Write the surrounding prose like a person

Use plain, specific language. Prefer a concrete observation, decision, or
example over generic claims. Vary sentence length naturally, remove filler, and
avoid inflated labels such as “world-class” unless an independent fact supports
them.

Do not use formulaic openings, repetitive three-part lists, stock transitions,
or claims that a page is “transformative,” “immersive,” or “cutting-edge”
without showing what actually happened. First-person writing must sound
consistent with Dr. Sahoo’s teaching and research voice and must not invent a
personal experience.

AI-assisted editing may be described as human-quality or human-reviewed only
after a person has reviewed it. Never claim that AI-assisted copy is “100%
human-generated.”

## 3. Design for every reader and screen

Every public experience must work from a 320-pixel phone screen through wide
desktop layouts, in portrait and landscape orientation, at 200% browser zoom,
with keyboard-only navigation, and with reduced motion enabled.

The minimum release checks are:

- no horizontal page scrolling at 320, 375, 768, 1024, and 1440 pixels;
- readable line lengths and fluid headings;
- controls with clear labels, visible focus, and comfortable touch targets;
- content order that still makes sense in one column;
- responsive images, tables, charts, dialogs, and navigation;
- sufficient contrast in light and dark themes;
- no meaning conveyed by colour or motion alone;
- print-friendly teaching material where practical.

## 4. Verify before publishing

Before release:

1. confirm facts against the named primary evidence;
2. validate complete corpus counts and sequential numbering;
3. run lint and a production build;
4. test representative routes and every shared layout at the target widths;
5. review the final rendered prose rather than relying on an AI detector;
6. obtain human sign-off for authorship-sensitive or devotional commentary.
