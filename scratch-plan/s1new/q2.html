Question 02 · Collis & Rukstad (2008)

Why do firms need one?

The blunt answer: without a strategy, every function optimises locally and the firm competes on whatever the loudest customer asked for most recently. Collis and Rukstad (2008) make the point diagnostically — most executives cannot state their firm’s strategy in a single clear sentence, and a strategy nobody can state cannot guide anybody’s choices.

Element 1

Objective

A single measurable end, with a deadline. Not “be the leading provider” — leading on what, by when?

Element 2

Scope

Where you will and will not compete: customer, geography, product, stage of the value chain. Scope is where the word no appears.

Element 3

Advantage

Why the chosen customer picks you and keeps picking you — the customer-facing promise and the activities that make it deliverable.

What a strategy statement is not

  • Mission — why the organisation exists.
  • Vision — what it wants to become.
  • Values — how people are expected to behave.

All three are useful and none of them is a strategy. They do not tell a product manager which segment to drop. The test is simple: if the sentence cannot be used to refuse an opportunity, it is not doing strategic work.

Draft one, in 35 words

Pick any firm you know well. Fill all three and read it back.
