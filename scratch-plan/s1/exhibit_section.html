Is the chief executive right?

It is early 2018. You sit on the board of an established carmaker. Your chief executive says: “Tesla loses money on every car it sells. We are profitable. There is nothing here to learn.” You have the case exhibits in front of you. Decide.

Finding 1

Ordinary at the gross line

Tesla's gross margin is 18.9% against an incumbent median of 17.7% — below Honda's 22.4% and Daimler's 20.0%. Whatever is happening here, it is not that Tesla builds cars unusually profitably.

Finding 2

The loss is a spending choice

From 18.9% gross to −13.7% operating is a fall of 32.6 points. R&D alone is 11.7% of revenue, 2.3× the 5.1% median — on a revenue base one-tenth the size. That is a decision, not a manufacturing failure.

Finding 3

Meanwhile it took the segment

Model S volume rose 76% from 2014 to 2016 while nine established rivals fell 25% between them, in a segment that shrank 8%. Share went from 16.7% to 31.9%.

What the numbers do and do not settle

The chief executive is reading an income statement as if it were a strategy. The loss is what holding the position costs; the volume shift is what it buys. Whether that trade is worth making is the argument — that it is a trade rather than incompetence is the finding.

The opposite case is also defensible, and you should be able to make it: a firm losing 13.7% at the operating line on one-tenth the revenue has proved nothing durable, and one model in one segment in one country is thin evidence. Note that the BMW 7-Series rose 33% over the same period.
