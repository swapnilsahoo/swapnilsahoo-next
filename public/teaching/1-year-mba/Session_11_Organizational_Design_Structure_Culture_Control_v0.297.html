Session 11 · Organizational Design: Structure, Culture & Control

The strategy that made you successful
is the structure that makes you fail next.

Organizational inertia: a structure optimized for yesterday's environment becomes a cage the moment the market shifts. If your structure made you successful, why would you ever change it — and how would you even notice it was time to?

First Principle Question

Strategy is the brain of the firm; structure is the body. A brilliant strategic decision, routed through the wrong structure, arrives at the front line distorted or too late to matter. Before you evaluate any company's strategy, ask: does its structure let that strategy actually reach a customer — or does it die in a committee three layers down?

Matching Structure to Strategy

First Principle Question

A matrix structure gives every employee two bosses on paper, to balance two priorities the firm cares about equally. Why does this "balanced" design so often produce paralysis instead of balance in practice?

Functional structure

Grouped by discipline (marketing, engineering, finance). Efficient for a single-product, single-market firm — deep specialization, clear career ladders. Breaks down fast once you have multiple product lines competing for the same functional resources.

Multidivisional (M-form)

Self-contained divisions (by product or geography), each with its own functions, evaluated on P&L. Scales well for diversified firms; risk is duplicated overhead and divisions optimizing locally at the expense of the whole company.

Matrix structure

Dual reporting lines (e.g., by function AND by product). In theory, balances competing priorities. In practice, ambiguous authority slows decisions exactly when speed matters most — the classic failure mode this session's debate should surface.

Simple/flat structure

Founder-centric, minimal hierarchy. Fast decisions, high adaptability — the default at most startups. Doesn't scale past a certain headcount without someone deliberately re-designing it, which many founders resist doing.
