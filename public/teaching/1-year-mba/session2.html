Official Session 05 · Internal Analysis & Resource-Based View

Two firms face the same industry.
One wins for a decade. Why?

Sessions 03–04 looked outward, at the industry. This session flips the lens inward: the resource-based view's core claim is that firms in the same industry differ systematically because they are unique, non-tradeable bundles of resources and capabilities — and that difference, not the industry structure, is often what explains who wins.

The Assigned Reading

Collis, D. J., & Montgomery, C. A. (1995). "Competing on Resources," Harvard Business Review. Their five market tests for whether a resource is genuinely valuable: is it hard to copy, does it depreciate slowly, who really captures the value it creates, can it be substituted, and is it truly superior to a rival's version — not just different. Most resources fail at least one test; the ones that pass all five are rare enough to be worth building a strategy around.

Case · Elon Musk at Tesla (2024)

The VRIO Test, Applied

Vertical manufacturing integration

Valuable: yes — controls quality and cost. Rare: yes — legacy automakers outsource heavily. Inimitable: partially — requires years of capital and learning to replicate. Organized: yes — Tesla built management systems around it from day one. Verdict: a genuine, if eroding, sustained advantage.

Supercharger network

Valuable: yes — range anxiety reduction. Rare: was, until Tesla opened it to rivals in 2023–24. Inimitable: no longer exclusive by design choice, not competitive failure. Organized: yes. Verdict: this resource's competitive value was deliberately traded away for ecosystem scale — a reminder that VRIO status is a choice, not just a fact.
