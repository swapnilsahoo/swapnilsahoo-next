Official Session 07 · Sustaining Competitive Advantage

You found an edge.
What stops the next competitor from finding it too?

Building advantage (Session 06) is only half the problem. Sustaining it requires either deterrence — a commitment so costly to reverse that rivals never bother trying to match it — or capabilities that compound faster than any rival can build from scratch.

The Assigned Reading

Ghemawat, P., & Pisano, G. P. (1997). "Sustaining Superior Performance: Commitments and Capabilities," Harvard Business School Note. Their point: a credible commitment deters imitation precisely because it would be expensive for the firm itself to reverse — that expense is the signal a rival reads before deciding whether to compete head-on at all.

Case · Patagonia: "Earth Is Now Our Only Shareholder"

A Commitment No Public Rival Can Match

A publicly listed competitor attempting to copy Patagonia's ownership transfer would trigger shareholder lawsuits and a stock-price penalty. That's deterrence working as designed: the imitation isn't just hard, it's structurally unavailable to firms with a different ownership form — which is exactly why it protects the advantage longer than a marketing campaign ever could.
